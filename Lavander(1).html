Lavender

Lavandula angustifolia

A fragrant, sun-loving herb with purple flower spikes and silvery-green leaves. Lavender brings colour to the garden and a gentle scent to drawers, crafts and quiet moments.

Give it sunshine, well-drained soil and a little room to grow. Once established, it is wonderfully low-maintenance.

How to grow lavender

Lavender prefers conditions that feel a little like the Mediterranean: plenty of light, soil that drains easily, and no overwatering.

☀

Sunshine

Choose a spot with at least 8 hours of direct sun each day. More sun usually means stronger growth and more flowers.

◯

Well-drained soil

Light, sandy or gravelly soil is ideal. If your soil stays wet, grow lavender in a raised bed or a pot with drainage holes.

♧

Water lightly

Water young plants while they establish. Mature lavender is drought-tolerant, so let the soil dry between waterings.

✂

Prune after flowering

Trim off spent flower stems and lightly shape the plant. Avoid cutting deeply into old, woody growth.

♧

Give it space

Leave room around each plant for air to circulate. This helps foliage dry and keeps the plant healthier.

❀

Be patient

Lavender may take a season to settle in. Once established, it can return with fragrant flowers year after year.

Care through the year

Lavender in every season

Choose a season for a quick care tip.

✿

Spring

Trim away any winter-damaged stems and give your plant a sunny spot with freely draining soil. Avoid heavy feeding; lavender prefers lean soil.

A little lavender goes a long way

Ways to use it

  • Tie dried stems into small bunches for a room or wardrobe.
  • Add dried buds to handmade sachets and craft projects.
  • Enjoy the colour and fragrance in borders, pots and pollinator gardens.

When to harvest

For fragrant bunches, cut flower stems when the first buds have opened, usually on a dry morning. Gather a small bunch and hang it upside down somewhere airy and out of direct sunlight.

Leave plenty of flowers for bees and other pollinators.

Garden note: Lavender is loved by bees. Avoid spraying flowers with pesticides, and let some blooms remain for visiting pollinators.
